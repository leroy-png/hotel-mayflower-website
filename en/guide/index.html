Museums

Leiden has thirteen museums. These are the ones you can reach on foot from the hotel.

± 3 min walk

Molenmuseum De Valk

A working seven-storey windmill from 1743 on the edge of the old town. Climb to the balcony for the best free-standing view over Leiden.

Walking route → Website →

± 4 min walk

Museum De Lakenhal

Leiden's own museum, in the seventeenth-century cloth hall: Rembrandt's earliest work, Lucas van Leyden, and the story of the city's cloth trade.

Walking route → Website →

± 5 min walk

Wereldmuseum Leiden

One of Europe's oldest ethnographic collections (until recently Museum Volkenkunde), telling the stories of cultures worldwide.

Walking route → Website →

± 9 min walk

Rijksmuseum Boerhaave

Five centuries of Dutch science and medicine, from the first microscopes to the anatomical theatre. Surprisingly gripping, even if science is not your thing.

Walking route → Website →

± 10 min walk

Rijksmuseum van Oudheden

The national museum of antiquities: an entire Egyptian temple stands in the entrance hall, plus Greek, Roman and Dutch archaeology.

Walking route → Website →

± 10 min walk

Japanmuseum SieboldHuis

The canal house where Philipp von Siebold displayed the objects he brought back from Japan in 1832: Japanese art in a Dutch merchant's house.

Walking route → Website →

± 12 min walk

Hortus botanicus Leiden

The oldest botanical garden in the Netherlands (1590), where Clusius planted the tulips that started Dutch tulip mania.

Walking route → Website →

± 22 min walk

Naturalis Biodiversity Center

Dinosaurs, including T. rex 'Trix', and the natural history of the planet. A little further out; bus 400 or a 20-minute walk past the station.

Walking route → Website →

Historic Leiden

The city is small enough to see properly in an afternoon. A short walk in this order makes a good route: Burcht, Hooglandse Kerk, Stadhuis, Rapenburg, Pieterskerk.

± 10 min walk

Burcht van Leiden

An eleventh-century circular fortress on an artificial mound where the Old and New Rhine meet. Free to enter, and the rampart walk gives you the rooftops of the whole old town.

Walking route → Website →

± 12 min walk

Pieterskerk

The great Gothic church where the Pilgrims worshipped before sailing for America; their leader John Robinson is buried here. Leiden is where the Mayflower story begins, and the hotel is named after that ship.

Walking route → Website →

± 10 min walk

Hooglandse Kerk

A soaring late-Gothic church, light and almost empty inside, with a monument to the burgomaster who kept the city fed during the 1574 siege.

Walking route → Website →

± 10 min walk

Rapenburg

Often called the most beautiful canal in the Netherlands: wide water, lime trees and the seventeenth-century houses of professors and merchants. The university's oldest building stands here.

Walking route → Website →

± 8 min walk

Leiden's hofjes

Leiden has around 35 hidden almshouse courtyards, built from the 1400s onward for elderly residents. Push open an unmarked door and you find a silent garden. Please keep your voice down; people live there.

Walking route → Website →

± 8 min walk

Stadhuis & Koornbrug

The Renaissance town hall façade on the Breestraat, and round the corner the covered Koornbrug where grain was traded out of the rain.

Walking route → Website →

Day trips

Keukenhof & the flower fields

The world's largest spring garden, in Lisse. Keukenhof is a spring attraction only: the 2027 season runs 18 March to 9 May 2027. The bulb fields around it are usually at their best from mid-April to early May.

Getting there: bus 854 (Keukenhof Express) leaves directly from Leiden Centraal, about four times an hour, roughly 25–30 minutes to the entrance. Combination tickets including entry are sold online. The bus runs during the Keukenhof season only.

Season dates checked August 2026; check keukenhof.nl before you travel.

Keukenhof website →

Formula 1 at Zandvoort

The Dutch Grand Prix runs 21–23 August 2026 at Circuit Zandvoort, and this is the final edition for now, as Zandvoort leaves the calendar in 2027.

Getting there: train from Leiden Centraal towards Haarlem and change for Zandvoort aan Zee; roughly an hour in total. On race days trains are very busy and extra services run, so travel early and buy tickets in advance.

Dates checked August 2026; confirm at dutchgp.com.

The beach

Katwijk aan Zee and Noordwijk are the closest stretches of North Sea coast: bus 31 or 32 from Leiden Centraal, around half an hour. Wide sand, dunes and a line of beach cafés in summer.

Parking: use Haagweg

The hotel has no private parking and the old centre was never built for cars. The easiest option is Parkeerterrein Haagweg (Haagweg 8), a 750-space car park just outside the centre.

Best of all, it runs a free shuttle bus into town every few minutes, and it will drop you where you ask, including at the Beestenmarkt.

  • Shuttle Mon–Wed 06:00–24:00
  • Thu & Fri 06:00–02:00
  • Sat 07:00–02:00
  • Sun 09:00–24:00

Tariff at the time of writing: first 15 minutes free, up to 90 minutes €4.50, then €1.50 per half hour, maximum €21.00 per day.

Tariffs and times checked August 2026; operators change them from time to time. Current rates at centrumparkeren.nl.
